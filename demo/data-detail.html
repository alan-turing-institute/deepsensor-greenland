HIRHAM5 regional climate model

HIRHAM5 is a regional climate model developed at the Danish Meteorological Institute (DMI), combining the dynamics of the HIRLAM weather forecast model with the physics of the ECHAM5 global climate model. Over Greenland it runs on a 5.5 km grid, driven at its boundaries by the ERA-Interim reanalysis, and is coupled to a snow and firn scheme that simulates surface melt, refreezing and runoff (Langen et al., 20171).

The model is used to generate predictions of surface melt across the entire grid, based on a set of input variables.

Following Schlager et al. (2026)2, our AI model predicts surface melt across the whole grid from the following inputs:

Surface melt
Melt on the prediction day, sampled at a sparse set of context points.
Atmospheric drivers
Latent and sensible heat flux, longwave and shortwave radiation, rainfall, snowfall and air temperature, over the prediction day and the 9 days before it.
Climatology
Rolling 10-year averages of rainfall, snowfall and air temperature.
Static and time features
Greenland ice mask, grid x/y coordinates and day of year (as sine/cosine).

HIRHAM5 ERA-Interim dataset, provided by the Danish Meteorological Institute, spans the period 1979–2018.

PROMICE weather station network

The Programme for Monitoring of the Greenland Ice Sheet (PROMICE), run by the Geological Survey of Denmark and Greenland (GEUS), operates automatic weather stations that record near-surface meteorology and surface energy and mass balance on the ice sheet. Together with the GC-NET network, it spans 52 active and historical stations across the ice sheet, peripheral glaciers and land areas (Fausto et al., 20263).

We subset 42 PROMICE stations overlapping the HIRHAM5 Greeland ice mask. We use their locations for model training and sensor placement.
